A shop online and a till at the counter, on one system

RA8M helps businesses in Iraq sell. Some of them sell from an Instagram feed and need a real shop behind the link. Some stand at a counter all day and need a till that keeps up. I designed both ends: RA8M Store, the storefront and the admin that runs it, and RA8M POS, the point of sale for a physical shop, with one design system underneath.

    Context

    One company, two ways to sell.

    RA8M is a commerce platform for businesses in Iraq. Its customers are small: a skincare seller with an Instagram following, a pharmacy, a cafe that bakes its own stock, an electronics shop. Almost none of them had software before. They had a phone, a notebook and a lot of conversations.

    Selling online in Iraq mostly means posting a product, answering “how much” in the comments, confirming size and colour over a call, and sending it with cash on delivery. Selling in a shop means a cashier at a counter, a paper bill, and an owner who checks stock from memory. RA8M gives each of those a proper tool, and keeps them on the same system so a business can start with one and add the other.

    The Store is the shop and the admin that runs it. The POS is the till and the back office behind the till. They share the design system, the way a customer is identified, and the idea that every order has both a fulfilment state and a payment state.

    Sell online

    A storefront on web and mobile with variants, cart and checkout, and an admin for orders, coupons and stock. One link to drop into a bio or a story.

    Sell at the counter

    A cashier screen that completes a sale in a few taps, and an Admin Panel for inventory, purchases, finance and reports. Tablet, phone and desktop.

    One system under both

    The same tokens, the same components, the same rules about customers and statuses. A business that outgrows one half does not relearn the other.

    Two products, one platform

    The same business, from the feed to the counter.

    A seller who starts on Instagram and a shop that starts at a counter are often the same business a year apart. RA8M was designed so that neither has to leave the system when they grow.

    What they share

    The decisions that were made once and used twice.

    Most of what makes the two products feel like one company was decided before either had a finished screen.

    One library

    Colour, type, spacing and components live in one Figma library. The storefront is built entirely from it, and the POS extends it rather than forking it.

    A customer is a phone number

    In Iraq that is how a shop knows a regular. Checkout asks for it first, the till types into it first, and everything else about the customer is optional.

    Two statuses per order

    Fulfilment and payment are tracked side by side in the Store admin and in the POS ledger. One merged status would hide exactly the orders that need attention.

    Local by default

    IQD pricing, +964 numbers, Baghdad addresses, cash on delivery and Arabic alongside English, in both products, not as a later localisation pass.

    Variants all the way down

    Sizes, colours, units and batches are part of how stock is structured, so the product card, the order row and the till line all follow the same model.

    Small swappable parts

    A drawer that holds product and cart. A shell where only the product grid and the quantity control change per shop. The reusable part is large; the special part is small.

    How it was built

    System first, screens second, and code where the screens repeat.

    Both products followed the same order of work. The pieces that repeat the most were the first to be settled, and the dashboard with the most repeated parts was written as code before it was drawn.

    1. Learn how each business sells

      Instagram sellers, a pharmacy counter, a cafe that makes its own stock. What a sale is, who stands where, and what the owner needs to know at the end of the day.

    Who decided what

    What AI did

    Wrote the analytics page as markup on the RA8M tokens, and drafted copy and placeholder catalogues for the mock data.

    What stayed with people

    Every rule about customers, statuses and stock, what swaps per shop and what stays, every component in the library, and every cut. I decided what each screen shows and in what order.

    Where it went wrong

    The first home page tried to do too much

    An earlier Store home concept with hero banners and deep category rows is still in the file. It looked like a marketplace, not a shop. The shipped version puts products first and keeps the rest small.

    Units arrived late

    The POS was first designed with plain quantities. Two dated rounds added units of measure and then variants, and each one reached back into the cart line, the bill and the stock page.

    Four shops looked like four products

    The temptation was to design the pharmacy and the cafe separately. Lining the four up in one table was what showed the shell was the same and only the quantity control and the product grid differed.

    What I would do next, across RA8M

    01

    Join the two halves

    A business that sells online and at the counter should see one inventory and one customer list. The models already match; the product that connects them does not exist yet.

    02

    Watch real sellers and cashiers

    An Instagram seller setting up their first ten products, and a cashier in a queue with a scanner and a flaky connection. Both designs were reviewed, neither has been tested against the real thing.

    03

    An Arabic-first pass

    Both products support Arabic, but they were designed in English first. A pass that starts from the right-to-left layout would catch what mirroring alone misses.
