Business banking a small business owner can open in ten minutes

Novo set out to build business banking for US small businesses. I was the first designer, so the mobile app, the web app, the admin tools and the design system all started as blank files on my screen.

The problem

Opening a US business account meant three to four hours at a branch and weeks of waiting. Then $15 to $25 a month in fees, a $1,500 minimum balance and no view of the business's financial health.

    Context

    Banking was built for branches. Small business owners live on their phones.

    Novo entered the US market with a plain ambition: democratise banking for small business owners, a segment the big banks had neglected for years with slow, expensive, branch-first service that put the bank's convenience ahead of the customer's.

    The cost of that neglect was easy to measure. Owners were spending three to four hours at a branch just to open an account, time they could not afford. Once open, they paid $15 to $25 a month, kept $1,500 or more locked up as a minimum balance, and still had no idea how healthy the business was. Banking felt like a burden, not a tool.

    What they had
    • Account opening took weeks
    • Monthly fees of $15 to $25
    • Minimum balance requirements
    • Hidden fees and transaction limits
    • Hours sitting in a branch
    • No real-time financial insight
    What we built
    • Ten minutes to apply, open in three days
    • No monthly fees
    • No minimum balance
    • Transparent pricing, unlimited transactions
    • Banking from a phone
    • A Smart Insights dashboard

    What the product had to do

    Spearhead the shift to online business banking in the US by putting every banking need on one platform, speeding up cash flow with transparent financial insight, making it secure, charging no fees and no minimums, and giving owners control through Smart Insights, virtual debit cards, express transfers and a customer success team that actually answered.

    How I found out

    Sixty interviews before a single screen.

    I ran sixty in-depth interviews with small business owners, freelancers and startup founders. The screen was deliberate: owners, founders or entrepreneurs with heavy banking needs (ten or more transactions a month), aged 18 to 60, already using a mobile banking app, and based in the US.

    75%
    Open the app ten to twelve times a day. Access had to be fast and frictionless; the home screen is the product.
    11%
    Were satisfied with how they opened their last account. This became the north star. If onboarding stayed broken, nothing else would matter.
    99%
    Called FDIC insurance and in-app security controls non-negotiable. Trust first, then speed.
    82%
    Wanted integrations with tools like Xero, QuickBooks and payment processors. One roof, not five tabs.
    55%
    Preferred mobile, while 35%, usually older owners, preferred the web. We had to be good at both.

    Who we designed for

    Julian, 28, founder of an SEO consultancy in New York.

    Julian left a Fortune 500 company four years in to start the consultancy. He manages four people, spends most of the week in client meetings and winning new work, and writes a tech blog on weekends. Tech-savvy, ambitious, and he values efficiency above everything.

    Goals

    • Deliver fast, expert service while staying current with technology
    • Grow the business, not the admin
    • See the finances without hiring an accountant

    Pain points

    • Hours lost at the bank to open an account, for a limited product at a high price
    • No time to visit a branch for deposits, cheques and transfers
    • New to running a business and often low on balance because nothing shows him where the money goes

    Needs

    • Smart, affordable banking that does not cost more money to run
    • Payroll, deposits and cheques from the phone, without a branch
    • Something that behaves like a financial adviser and keeps track of spending

    "I want to spend my time growing my business, not waiting in line at the bank."

    Julian, persona

    What owners said, thought, did and felt

    Says

    "I hate being charged when I don't maintain the minimum balance." "I like talking to people when I visit the bank, it feels safer than online." "I wish something kept track of all my finances and gave me insights."

    Thinks

    Gets lost in the app doing a basic transfer. Worries about data safety on the mobile app. Feels that chat support is not a real person. Appreciates how easy it is to send money, even when the app is not.

    Does

    Checks the balance on the phone all day. Pays bills from the app or web. Keeps the minimum balance even when money is short, to avoid the fee. Hates going to the branch for small things.

    Feels

    Annoyed by the monthly fee. Anxious about a bank with no branches. Guilty about using a bank that only thinks about deposits. Grateful for the time online banking gives back.

    The journey, the flows and the opportunity tree

    Before any screens, I mapped Julian's journey from first hearing about Novo to ending the relationship, drew the user flows for the three jobs that mattered most, and ran an opportunity tree to decide what belonged in the first release. Tap any of them to see it larger.

    What I did

    Four principles, then two decisions that shaped everything else.

    Speed over everything

    Every interaction should be faster than the traditional alternative.

    Transparency first

    No hidden fees, no surprises. People always know what they are paying.

    Security without friction

    FDIC-insured trust with modern, convenient access.

    Insight over data

    Don't just show the numbers. Say what they mean for the business.

    Decision 1: a ten-minute onboarding

    ChallengeTraditional banks required fifteen or more fields and a branch visit, and took weeks.
    ApproachProgressive disclosure. Ask only what is legally required up front and defer optional fields to after the account exists.
    SolutionCut the initial form from 23 fields to 8. Added smart defaults and document scanning from the camera.
    Result65% completion against an industry average of 45% at first launch, rising to 73% after the iterations below.

    Decision 2: the Smart Insights dashboard

    Challenge82% of owners wanted financial insight, but every banking app showed them a transaction list.
    ApproachCategorise transactions automatically and show spending visually, with trends over time.
    SolutionA dashboard for cash flow, spending by category, income against expenses, and proactive low-balance alerts.
    ResultOne in five users engaged with insights weekly, and overdraft incidents fell 15%.

    The first pass was low-fidelity on purpose: nine wireframes covering the dashboard, transfers, cheque capture and payees, tested before any visual design. The production screens at the top of this page are where they ended up.

    With the team

    Remote across three continents, reviewed screen by screen.

    A fully remote team across the US, Europe and Asia, with product and engineering in New York. For the first two years I was the only designer; two more joined after that, and I moved into leading the team while still reviewing every screen before it shipped. I also sat in product planning and helped set company-wide OKRs, which is how the onboarding completion rate became a company number rather than a design one.

    The design system grew out of the same pressure. It started as a way to keep one designer consistent across iOS, Android, web, the marketing site and admin, and became the thing that let a bigger team move fast without drift.

    How we checked

    Ten people, the three core tasks, think-aloud and a SUS score.

    After the first design iteration I ran usability testing on both the mobile app and the web app, with ten participants matching the persona. The sessions were task-based, covering account opening, transfers and the insights dashboard, with a think-aloud protocol and System Usability Scale scoring.

    What changed

    Respect people's time at the first interaction and they trust you with their money.

    Account opening completion reached 73%, with an average of eight minutes to open. Thirty-day retention settled at 89%, the App Store rating at 4.5, support tickets fell 42% and NPS reached +67. Novo grew from an idea into a category-defining fintech on a system that held across five surfaces, and the onboarding funnels and landing pages became a steady source of activation.

    What I'd do differently

    Three things that worked, three I would change.

    What worked

    Early and frequent research. Sixty interviews up front gave us conviction and let us push back on stakeholder assumptions. When the CEO wanted five more onboarding fields "for better data", we had the evidence for why that would hurt conversion.

    Clear principles. "Speed over everything" settled debates fast. When we disagreed about complexity we asked whether it made the user faster. If not, we cut it.

    Cross-platform in parallel. Designing mobile and web together rather than porting later meant the 35% who preferred the web never felt second-class.

    What I would change

    Bring compliance in at week one, not week six. We designed a clean onboarding flow and then had to add three screens for KYC. Designing around the constraints from the start would have been better than retrofitting.

    Test accessibility, not just completion. We never recruited participants with disabilities, and post-launch feedback about contrast and screen readers showed the cost. It should have been built in.

    Stay low-fidelity longer. We went high-fidelity too early and grew attached to work that testing later undid. Two sprints of rougher prototypes would have saved three weeks of rework.

    What the team asked for next

    Predictive cash flow, because the question every owner asked was "will I have enough for payroll next Friday?". Team accounts with role-based permissions, which did not make the first release. And deeper integrations with Gusto, Stripe and Shopify, so Novo could be the command centre rather than one more tab.
