    Context

    One register for the cafe, the shop and the service van.

    Piggy Bank POS is an all-in-one point of sale for retailers, food service and service businesses. It takes payments in person, runs inventory, customers, reporting and staff controls, and ties into online sales. Merchants can run it as a regular POS or switch on the optional Cash Discount Program, built to recoup most of the card processing cost. All of it lives on a single portrait canvas, 720 by 1280, on a terminal with an HD touchscreen.

    The problem

    Wide product, short queue.

    A POS gets judged in the few seconds a customer waits. Make the common path feel instant and keep the long tail one deliberate step away.

    Every extra tap is a longer line, and every confusing total is a conversation nobody wants to have. The same screens had to work for a barista, a shopkeeper and a contractor.

    Add two commercial programs that change the math on every receipt, and the risk is a home screen that turns into an admin panel. Then there is delivery. A screen set this large drifts the moment it gets rebuilt by hand.

    What AI drafted

    First-pass markup, token mapping, component scaffolds and repeat QA walk-throughs. The slow, mechanical parts of getting a screen from file to browser.

    What I decided

    Component boundaries, what counts as one reusable button, touch sizes, the discount states, and what was good enough to ship. Anything I would not sign off on went back.

    Iterations

    Change got cheap.

    Small refinements made a big difference. The inventory keypad now gives price entry more visibility, and Past Orders presents transactions in a cleaner, better organised layout with clearer payment details.

    Keypad and totals

    The inventory price got a dedicated green input area, which made it far more visible and gave price entry, totals and the keypad a clearer hierarchy.

    Past Orders

    The order list was reorganised with date-based grouping, a date-range filter, clearer payment statuses and more structured transaction details for easier scanning.

    The design

    The app experience, screen by screen.

    Every screen sits on the same portrait canvas and the same green. What changes is how much a cashier has to read before acting, which is as little as possible on the selling screens and a bit more in the back office.

    Outcome

    Real impact, measured at the counter.

    With Piggy Bank POS the client reported better efficiency, smoother operations and a more streamlined checkout.

    What I would change

    Three things I would do next.

    Let usage pick the drawer

    Four drawer slots were a judgement call. Six weeks of tap data per merchant would show which of the 23 tiles earn a slot, and the drawer could differ for a cafe and a contractor.

    Test the receipt, not just the screen

    The Cash Discount Program changes what a customer reads on paper. The next round should put real receipts in front of real customers before the next total is touched.

    Close the loop in the file

    The browser agent reports mismatches back as text. The next step is to have it land them as comments on the Figma frame, so the fix starts where the source is.
