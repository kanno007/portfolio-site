    Context

    Every publication had grown its own habits.

    The same button could mean three different things across three titles, and each change meant three separate conversations between design and engineering. The ask was a shared language, introduced underneath products that could not pause.

    How I found out

    Research before commitment.

    Started with an audit of patterns across the publications: what each title had, what it called it, and where the same component meant different things. Then agreed naming, ownership and the order of migration with the design and engineering leads, so the system had signatures before it had swatches.

    What I did

    Tokens as the contract between design and engineering.

    Led the design system strategy. Introduced design tokens as the shared contract, so a decision made once could flow to every publication. Unified components and patterns across the core products and migrated screen by screen, without disrupting anything already live.

    A change made once could flow to every publication, without a redesign project.

    With the team

    Naming, ownership, and the order of migration.

    Worked remotely with the design and engineering leads across the publications to agree on naming, ownership and sequencing. Later I moved into the subscriptions product team as product and service designer.

    What changed

    Less handoff friction, faster implementation, and a lighter touch on paying customers.

    Handoff friction dropped and implementation sped up across teams. In subscriptions, I shipped improvements to existing subscriber lifecycle flows instead of rebuilding them, which is usually the right call for a product that already has paying customers.
