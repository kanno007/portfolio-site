Luna: one design system for ten publications

SPH Media is Singapore's largest media group: The Straits Times, The Business Times, Lianhe Zaobao, Berita Harian, Tamil Murasu and more, plus a shared subscription platform serving millions of readers. I led Luna, the token-first design system that gave all of them one language, built while every title kept publishing.

Role
Lead Designer, Luna Design System (product design consultant)
Timeline
Sep 2023 to Sep 2024
Scope
Tokens, type, colour, spacing and grid, 400+ components, documentation, governance, WCAG 2.1 AA
Team
10 designers and 8 engineers across the publications, remote with Singapore
In thirty seconds

The problem

Ten publications, each with its own design language, component library and front-end code. The same subscription flow looked different on The Straits Times and Berita Harian, and accessibility could not be enforced anywhere.

What I did

Designed Luna token-first: type, colour, spacing and grid as tokens, then 400+ components across 50 categories, with documentation, a contribution model and the handoff tooling to match.

What changed

About 30% less time on repetitive UI work, ten publications on Luna in production, 18 designers and engineers on one system, and new-product design groundwork cut from weeks to days.

    Objective

    Ten publications, ten ways of doing the same thing.

    Each SPH publication had grown its own design language, component library and front-end codebase. As the group accelerated its digital transformation, that fragmentation became the bottleneck: product teams moved slowly, readers got inconsistent experiences, and accessibility was nearly impossible to enforce at scale. Luna was created to solve this.

    Inconsistent
    A subscription flow on The Straits Times looked and behaved differently from the same flow on Berita Harian, even though readers could hold accounts across both.
    Duplicated
    Designers on every team were rebuilding buttons, modals and form fields independently, each time from scratch.
    Slow
    A new product or feature needed three to four weeks of design groundwork before any real product work could start.
    Uneven
    With no central standard, accessibility depended on the team. Some publications were WCAG-compliant, others were not.
    Error-prone
    No shared tokens or naming meant every handoff was a negotiation, and a slow one.
    My role

    Strategy, tokens, governance, and the alignment to make them stick.

    As Lead Designer on Luna I owned the design strategy, the token architecture, component governance and cross-team alignment. In practice that meant defining the foundations (tokens, type scale, colour palettes, grids), leading the design and documentation of the 400+ components, setting up the contribution model for how the system evolves, running alignment sessions with publication brand teams, front-end engineers and product managers, holding every component to WCAG 2.1 AA, and owning the design-to-development handoff process and tooling.

    Scope and plan

    Three things to build, six steps to build them.

    The objective was a unified, reader-centric system that kept the experience consistent across every SPH product. We scoped it into three parts and worked through them in order.

    Foundation

    The design principles and guidelines every decision rests on, expressed as tokens so they hold across products.

    UI components

    Buttons, forms, navigation, modals and the rest, each built for consistency, accessibility and responsiveness.

    Documentation

    Guidelines, a central library and a process for updates, so designers and engineers stay in step.

    1. Research and analysisAudit of every publication's patterns and codebase
    2. Define the core systemPrinciples, naming, token architecture
    3. Build the component library400+ components with states and variants
    4. Design tokens and variablesFigma variables mirrored in code
    5. Documentation and handoffComponent details, usage rules, Code Connect
    6. LaunchRollout publication by publication
    Foundations

    Type, colour, space and grid as tokens, not as taste.

    Typography

    One family, Source Sans 3, with five weights and fifteen sizes from 80 down to 10. Two line heights, 115% for headings and 140% for paragraphs. On top of that sit semantic tokens such as main-title, body-text and button-text-md, each with a mobile and desktop value, so a designer picks a role rather than a number.

    Colour

    Blue is at the heart of SPH Media's brand, so the primary palette is two hues of it. Four secondary hues give publications warmth or calm where they need it, and a cool greyscale handles everything neutral. Every hue runs from 900 to 50, with contrast graded on each step so teams know which pairs are safe for text.

    400+ components, every state drawn, every state named.

    The library covers 50 categories. A few of the ones that carry the most weight in a subscription business are below. Each one ships with its states, sizes and variants, and the names match the code.

    Documentation and governance

    A component is not done until someone else can use it without asking.

    Every component has a details page with the same shape: an overview, when to use it, when not to, its structure in tokens, and its variants. Alongside that sits the Luna site itself, with the principles, the ecosystem of publications on the system, and the contribution model for proposing and reviewing changes.

    OverviewWhat the component is and the job it does, in two sentences.
    When to useThe situations it was designed for, and the alternative when it is not the right fit.
    StructureBackground, colour, border and padding expressed as tokens, so engineers map it without guessing.
    VariantsTypes, content, states and sizes, named exactly as they are in Figma and in code.
    With the team

    Remote with Singapore, ten designers and eight engineers.

    Luna was built with the publication teams, not for them. Alignment sessions with brand teams settled how far each title could flex the palette. Working sessions with front-end engineers settled token names and the handoff tooling. Product managers got a system they could plan against, because a new feature no longer started with three weeks of design groundwork.

    Efficiency

    Reusable components and tokens cut the time spent on repetitive design and development work by around 30%.

    Scalability

    New publications and features slot into the system instead of starting from a blank file.

    Reader experience

    One design language across titles, so readers navigate and engage the same way wherever they are.

    Accessibility

    WCAG 2.1 AA built into the components, which widened the audience the platform serves.

    The bigger change was in how the teams worked. For the first time a designer on The Straits Times and a developer on Berita Harian were working from the same language. Onboarding new designers got much faster. And when SPH launched a new publication or feature, Luna reduced the design groundwork from weeks to days.

    Summary

    The hard part of a design system is not the components.

    Luna is the connective tissue between SPH Media's products, publications and teams. A token-first architecture, a multi-publication theming model and a rigorous documentation and governance framework let SPH ship consistent, accessible, brand-coherent experiences at scale.

    The hardest part of design systems work is not building components. It is building alignment. Getting designers, engineers, brand teams and product managers to trust a shared system takes communication, patience, and a willingness to let other people shape what you are building.
