Research synthesis with Claude: what I keep and what I throw away

A first pass in minutes is only useful if you know which parts to distrust.

In this post
    Share

    After a round of interviews I hand Claude Code the transcripts and my own coding frame, the themes I already suspect, and ask for tagging, clustering and quotes. It comes back in minutes. Here is how I treat what comes back.

    Keep

    The clustering. It is good at finding the same complaint phrased five different ways, which is tedious for a person and the place where I used to miss things. The quote pulls, as long as I check every one against the transcript. The list of things nobody mentioned, which is often the most useful output.

    Throw away

    Any theme that only has one quote behind it. Any summary sentence that sounds more confident than the people did. Anything about why someone did something, because the model is guessing at motive and so was the participant.

    Never skip

    Reading the transcripts myself. All of them. Synthesis is where a designer’s judgment gets built, and outsourcing that is outsourcing the job. The tool makes the first pass faster. It does not make the second pass optional.
