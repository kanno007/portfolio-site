A form is a conversation, not a database

Lessons from KYC at Novo that apply to any sign-up form that loses people.

In this post
    Share

    Know-your-customer forms are where business banks lose customers. The regulator needs a lot of answers, and the easy design is a long page that mirrors the database. It is also the design that gets abandoned.

    One question at a time

    We broke the form into screens that asked one thing each. Total fields stayed the same. Completion went up anyway, because nobody was looking at forty empty boxes and deciding to come back later. Later never comes.

    Say it the way a person would

    “Legal entity type” became “How is your business set up?” with the options spelled out. Every bank term got a plain sentence under it. If the compliance team could not agree to the plain version, we kept the term and explained it right there, never in a help centre.

    Show the end from the start

    A progress indicator that tells the truth. Four steps means four steps. And every screen saved, so closing the app on the train did not mean starting over at the desk.

    None of this is clever. It is just treating the form as a conversation with someone who has better things to do, and designing for the moment they are most likely to leave.
